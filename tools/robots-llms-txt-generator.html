Managing Search & AI Crawlers Without Losing Organic Rankings

Search bots and AI scrapers obey distinct user-agent blocks inside robots.txt. Misconfigured wildcards (such as blocking /*? carelessly or listing Disallow: / for User-agent: * without explicit allows) can silently de-index revenue pages or waste crawl budget on faceted filter combinations.

Key Crawler Separation Strategy

  • Keep Googlebot, Bingbot, and OAI-SearchBot open on canonical content and tool pages so your brand appears in search results and live search citations.
  • Filter low-value scrapers & faceted parameters like /*?sort=* and CCBot to preserve origin bandwidth—detailed further in our Real-Time LLM Web Crawlers Guide.
  • Pair robots.txt with a single clean Sitemap Index rather than listing duplicate child sitemaps redundantly.

robots.txt & llms.txt FAQs

What is llms.txt and why do sites add it alongside robots.txt?

llms.txt is a lightweight Markdown index placed at /llms.txt that curates canonical guides and tools for AI retrieval engines.

How does robots.txt resolve conflicting Allow and Disallow rules?

Per RFC 9309, the longest matching path rule wins; if an Allow and Disallow rule match with equal length, Allow takes precedence.

Does blocking GPTBot or Google-Extended affect Google Search rankings?

No. Blocking Google-Extended controls Gemini training usage separately from Googlebot search indexing.
